## Introduction

Sound problems rarely begin with a formula. They begin with an experience: an unwanted hum, a vibration, unclear speech, noise passing through a wall, or an environment that simply does not sound right.

_Making Sense of Sound_ is a technical writing series that connects these real-world experiences with acoustic and vibration engineering. Rather than treating individual metrics, methods and standards as separate topics, the series follows four connected perspectives:

- **The Receiver** — how people hear, perceive and respond to sound.
- **The Path** — how acoustic and vibration energy propagates, couples and reaches the receiver.
- **The Measurement** — how signals are captured, processed, interpreted and validated.
- **The Space** — how rooms, buildings, outdoor environments and context shape the final experience.

Together, they form a practical engineering sequence:

> **Understand the experience → trace the physical mechanism → measure the evidence → improve the system or space**
>
## Author Background

I am a senior acoustic engineer with more than ten years of practical experience in noise, vibration and sound-quality engineering.

My work has covered automotive NVH, machinery, household appliances and complex acoustic systems, including acoustic measurement, signal analysis, structural dynamics, sound quality and transfer-path engineering.

Across these projects, I have learned that meaningful acoustic analysis requires more than measuring a sound level. It requires understanding the source, transmission path, receiver, operating condition and human response as one connected system.

This series brings those lessons together and extends the same engineering mindset to room, building, environmental and soundscape applications.
